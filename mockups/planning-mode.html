The planner engine already exists — usePlanner.ts holds a week-by-week plan with transfers, chips, hits and auto-lineup. Today it stores exactly one.

Concept 1 — the shelf

Each plan is the whole six weeks: who you transfer, when, what it costs in hits, and what it projects. Duplicating one and changing a single decision is the whole interaction.

Concept 2 — head to head

Running totals, with the honest band around them

Cumulative expected points, net of hits. The shaded band is the 10th-to-90th outcome range for the leading plan.

A · HoldB · One a weekC · Chase Chelsea10th–90th for plan B
The band is the point, and it is uncomfortable. The best plan beats the worst by 5.6 points over six weeks. The 10th-to-90th range within the best plan is 69 points. Plan choice is worth about a 8% sliver of what variance does — so a comparison tool that shows only the projected totals is inviting you to agonise over a rounding error. Showing both is what makes it honest, and it is the reason to build it: not to find the best plan, but to see how little the question matters and stop churning.

Concept 4 — the read

What the comparison concludes

Take plan B, and expect it not to matter much.

B projects 344.5 against A's 338.9 — worth 5.6 points over six weeks, or under a point a week, and it takes no hit to get there. Every one of its five moves is a free transfer used in a week where banking bought nothing.

Plan C does not pay. Doubling on Chelsea before Hull at home is the right read on the fixture — it gains 4.3 before the hit — but the −4 eats it, and it lands at 339.2, -5.3 behind B. A hit needs to buy about a point a week for six weeks to break even, and this one buys a third of that.

All three sit inside each other's range. B's 10th percentile (310) is below A's median (338). On any given six-week run the worst plan here beats the best about four times in ten. That is not an argument against planning — it is an argument for planning once and leaving it alone.

This is the bit that is actually hard to copy. A solver gives you the optimum and no sense of whether it is worth having. The reason to write the verdict is that "these plans are indistinguishable" is a genuine, actionable answer, and it is the one a tool built to sell transfers can never give.
Concept 5 — where plans live

Does this need an account? No.

Recommended

Browser storage + a shareable link

Plans sit in localStorage alongside the one the planner already keeps, and any plan encodes into a link you can send yourself.

  • No sign-up, no email, nothing to breach
  • A whole plan is about 60 characters — fifteen player ids at ten bits, eight transfers, five chip slots, base64url
  • The link moves a plan between phone and laptop, and into a WhatsApp group
  • Lost if the browser is cleared and no link was saved
Only if it earns it

Accounts

A login buys silent cross-device sync and server-side compute. It costs a database, auth, a privacy policy, and a category of risk this site has spent real effort avoiding.

  • Sync without having to keep a link
  • Plans survive a cleared browser
  • Personal data where there is currently none
  • A second thing to run, and to keep up at 3am
The site holds no account today and is better for it — docs/DOMAIN_CATEGORISATION.md leans on "nothing is for sale and no money changes hands" to argue its way out of a Gambling classification, and "we collect nothing" is the same argument in a stronger form. The link approach gets you every practical benefit of an account except silent sync, and silent sync is not worth a user database. If people start asking for it, that is the moment to reconsider — not before.
