The right-hand column, as tabs

The column now carries four things stacked: the plan library, the squad's verdict, the Squad Lab and the transfer market. On a full squad that is about 1,400 pixels of column against 660 of board — so three of the four are always scrolled past, and the market, which is the one you act with, is the one at the bottom. Tabs fix the order. What follows is four ways to cut them, then what I'd actually build.

Idea 1

Everything is a tab

Four tabs, equal billing: Players, Squad, Fixtures, Plans. The column becomes one panel with one job at a time.

The catch. Plans as a tab buries the switcher inside the panel you are using for something else — you cannot see which plan you are editing while you edit it, which is the one thing that strip exists to say. And the red dot on Squad is doing a lot of work: it is the only route to "Garner is injured and in your eleven", on a tab you have no reason to open.

Idea 2 · my recommendation

Plans stay put; the rest is tabbed

The library keeps its place at the top of the column, because it names the thing you are looking at. Below it, one tabbed panel: Market — the list you build and transfer with — Read, the verdict and the Lab, and Fixtures, which is new.

Idea 3

What "Fixtures" would actually hold

This is the tab that does not exist yet, so it is worth being concrete about. Your fifteen, six weeks out, coloured by difficulty — one row per player, sorted by how bad the run is. It answers the question the Horizon panel currently answers as a sentence.

Idea 4

The tab follows what you just did

Same three tabs as idea 2, but the panel switches itself: sell a player and it opens Market filtered to his position; step to a gameweek where three of your fifteen are in a hard fixture and it opens Fixtures.

Half of this exists already — selling a player does swing the market round to his position. The new part is the tab switch and the sentence saying why the list changed. The risk is a panel that moves when you did not ask it to, which is the most annoying thing an interface can do, so it should only ever fire on an action you took deliberately — a sale, never a scroll.

What I'd build

Idea 2, with idea 3 inside it

Plans stay pinned, three tabs below: Market · Read · Fixtures. Market is the default and keeps its sticky behaviour, so the thing you act with is never more than one click away and usually zero. Read collects the verdict and the Lab — they are one thought and they were two boxes. Fixtures is the new one, and it is worth building because the run your fifteen are walking into is currently a sentence in a panel rather than something you can look at.

I would not do idea 1: the plan you are editing has to stay visible while you edit it. And idea 4's auto-switching I would add only after the tabs have been lived with — it is a good idea that becomes a bad one the first time it fires when you did not mean it.
