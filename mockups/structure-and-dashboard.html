What ships, and what the home page becomes

Two proposals. First: the Squad Builder has grown 29 panels and needs an edit before it goes live. Second: the dashboard as a real home page for someone with their team ID saved.

Part one · the problem

29 panels, behind a tab, inside a tab

Every panel we built is good. That is exactly why this is a problem — nothing is obviously deletable, so it all stayed. A manager with four minutes before a deadline currently has to know which of six sub-tabs holds the answer.

The test I would apply: most managers, most weeks, need three answers — who do I captain, do I transfer, and is anyone in my team a problem. Everything else is depth for the twenty minutes on a Sunday, not the four minutes on a Friday. Right now those three answers live in three different sub-tabs, and none of them is the first thing you see.

Two levels. The six panels that change a decision are one scroll with no navigation at all; depth is one click away and never in the way.

The cut, panel by panel

Review (ships prominent) — Squad verdict · Since you last looked · Squad risk monitor · The armband week by week · What a transfer would actually buy · Squad Lab horizon lead.

Deep dive (collapsed) — Ladder · Floor and ceiling · Who is carrying this squad · Goal sources · Defence concentration · Minutes risk · Chip windows · Fixture turn map · Price watch · Ownership swing · Projected against actual.

Merge — “How much rides on one result” appears twice and “What a transfer would actually buy” appears twice (empty-state variants). Minutes risk is also the top driver in the risk monitor, so the standalone panel becomes a drill-down rather than a sibling.

My recommendation

A for the home page, and cut the Builder to two levels

Option A. It is the only one of the three that works on a phone without compromise, and phone is where a deadline gets handled. It also matches what the site already does better than anyone: telling you the answer and then showing the working. B is a better desktop tool but it is a tool — it assumes you arrive knowing what you want. C is the most original and I would keep the “before this deadline / decide next week / plan for later” grouping from it and fold that into A’s top card, which is roughly what the mock above already does.

The commercial argument for saving a team ID: right now every visit starts from zero. A saved ID turns the site from a thing you look up into a thing you check — and weekly habit is the only metric that matters for a free site that wants an audience. It costs one input box and a localStorage key, and it is the single highest-leverage change on this list.

What I would not do: put the 29 panels on the home page in a prettier grid. The problem is not the layout, it is that no-one has decided what matters. A dashboard that shows everything is the same wall with better spacing.
