Stepping through gameweeks

Five ways to replace the two arrows with something that shows the road ahead. Every figure is this squad's real projection, read straight out of the app by stepping the current control eight times.

‹Gameweek 1›

What is there now. It works, and it tells you nothing about GW6 until you have clicked five times to find it.

Idea 1

The bars are the buttons

A mini bar chart where each bar is the week's projected points — and clicking one goes there. The shape of the next two months is the navigation.

Strongest at showing where the good and bad weeks are. GW6 is visibly the trough and GW7 the peak before you have read a single number. Weakest on a phone: eight bars in 390px gives each one about 44px, which is at the edge of a comfortable tap target — it would want to scroll horizontally past six.

Idea 2

Rating rings

The GW rating you already compute, drawn as a ring. The number is the rating; the fill is the same thing so it reads at a glance.

Handsome, and it uses a figure the site already has and mostly hides. But the ratings barely move — 75 to 86 across eight weeks — so eight nearly-full rings look almost identical and the chart says less than the bars do. A ring is a good shape for a number that uses its whole range, and this one does not.

Idea 3 · my recommendation

A card per week

Gameweek, projected points, a bar for the shape, and the one thing that changes a decision — how many of your fifteen face a hard game.

This is the one I would build. It carries three readings at once and each is doing work the others cannot: the number is precise, the bar makes the comparison instant, and the hard count is the thing a projection hides — GW6 projects 50.8 and walks 7 of 15 into a hard game, which is a different kind of bad week from GW8's 51.7 with 6.

On a phone it scrolls. Eight of these will not fit 390px at a legible size and the first draft of this mock-up simply clipped them off the right edge — so the row snaps and scrolls, showing about four weeks at a time with the current one anchored left. That is a real cost: the whole window is no longer visible at once on the device where most deadlines are handled, which is the argument for idea 4 as the phone layout rather than this.

Idea 4

Segmented control

The plainest version. One row, eight cells, the number under each.

Cheapest to build and the least likely to be misread. It is also the least useful: with no bar and no colour, spotting that GW6 is the problem means comparing eight numbers by eye, which is exactly the work a chart is supposed to do for you. Worth keeping in mind as the mobile fallback for idea 3 rather than as a design of its own.

Idea 5

Timeline with events

A dotted line through the window, with markers where something actually happens — the wall, the peak, a chip worth playing.

The most editorial of the five, and the one that fits a season rather than a fortnight. It answers "when should I do something" instead of "how many points is this week", which is a better question — but it depends on having events worth marking, and outside a chip window or a fixture wall most weeks have none. A timeline of eight unmarked dots is worse than a bar chart. I would hold this until the chip and blank/double logic is richer, then revisit.

Recommendation: idea 3, with idea 4 as the phone layout. Keep the arrows — they are how you move one week at a time, and a strip does not replace that — but put the strip above them so the whole window is visible and one tap away. The figures here are the projection and the hard count rather than the GW rating, because those two vary enough to be worth drawing: across these eight weeks the projection moves 50.8 to 57.9 and the hard count 1 to 7, while the rating sits between 75 and 86 and calls seven of the eight weeks "strong".
