Squad compare & builder — seven concepts

Every number below is computed from today's site_data: two legal £100.0m fifteens, their per-gameweek expected points, and the four squad dimensions the site already scores. Nothing is placeholder.

One caveat worth stating: GW1 in the live app is priced off bookmaker odds, and these figures use the strength model for every week. GW1 here reads a touch differently from the app (Raya 4.62 against the app's 4.4); GW2 onward match.

Concept 1

The verdict, and the contradiction in it

Lead with the number people came for, then immediately qualify it — because on this pair the two headline numbers disagree, and that disagreement is the most useful thing on the page.

Plan B has the better players. Plan A scores more points.

This is the panel doing its job, not a bug. Squad rating averages what fifteen footballers are, over a season. The projection is what this fifteen is likely to return over these six weeks — quality multiplied by fixtures, minutes and who gets the armband. B rates 76 to A's 75 and still projects 1.7 fewer points, because A owns Haaland and captains him in seven of eight weeks. A comparison that showed only one of these numbers would be telling you half the story and sounding certain about it.

One honesty note that has to ship with this panel: these dimensions come from last season's percentiles, and pre-season they exist for 7 of the fifteen at the back and 8 up front — the rest are new signings and promoted players with no record. The mean is taken over the players who have one.

Concept 3

Where the gap opens, week by week

The timeline you asked for: expected points against gameweeks, both plans on one axis, the gap between them shaded. A total tells you which plan is ahead; this tells you when, which is the difference between a transfer and a rebuild.

Plan A Plan B Gap

Six weeks, and the lead changes hands three times. A wins GW3 and GW5 by around two points a week; B takes GW4 and GW6. Nothing here is a rebuild — the plans are within 1.7 points over six weeks, which is inside the noise of a single captain haul. The chart earns its place by saying that plainly instead of letting a 1.7-point total read as a verdict.

The rail is the point: the four numbers that should decide a pick are visible while you are making it, with the delta from your last change attached. The "−2, you traded quality for fixtures" line is the same contradiction Concept 1 opens with, caught at the moment it happens rather than explained afterwards.

On the screenshots you shared. What is worth taking from them is the premise — two plans side by side, one timeline, a clear winner per row. What is not worth taking is the execution: their comparison is a table of totals with a colour on the bigger number, which is exactly the design that would have told you "A wins by 1.7" and hidden GW6. So these concepts keep the side-by-side and the timeline, and differ deliberately in three ways: every panel carries the reason underneath it, near-ties are drawn as near-ties instead of being awarded, and the squad dimensions you already compute do the work a generic "rating" column does on theirs.
