Mockups · GW Review, on the gameweek that has just finished

The first review this site has ever been able to write

GW1 is played. Every number below is measured from projections/gw1.json against actuals/gw1.json — 495 players projected before the deadline, 310 who featured, joined on code rather than name because fourteen surnames are shared. Nothing here is illustrative.

The page already tells you what it should be. The current stub lists six things it promises: Team of the Week, the captain call, rode their luck, where the model missed, what the crowd did, and into the next one. That is the brief, and every direction below delivers all six — what differs is which one leads, because that decides what the page is for.

Two components already exist and nothing imports them. TeamOfTheWeek.tsx builds a best XI under a £100m constraint, and GameweekCover.tsx is a magazine front page. Direction 3 is mostly that second file, pointed backwards.

02

Report card first

Lead with the thing nobody else does. Every FPL site publishes a Team of the Week. Almost none publish how wrong they were, in public, every week, whether or not it flatters them — and this site has promised to twice already. So the page opens on its own marking, and the hauls follow. For: it is the strongest trust argument the site has and it costs nothing now the two files exist; it is also the one section that cannot be copied without having stored a projection before the deadline. Against: a page that opens on arithmetic is a page fewer people scroll, and in a week the model reads badly it is a rough front door.

The model's gameweek 1
1.66 mean absolute error, points per player

Across all 495 players it projected — not only the 299 who featured, because a player it expected and who never came on is a miss too. Mean error +0.22: very slightly low. Scoring only the players who played gives 2.29 and a much more flattering story about which part was wrong, so it is not the number published.

And the honest headline

It missed on minutes, not on goals

Sixteen players it projected 3+ did not play at all, worth 55 projected points. Forty-one it projected under 1.5 played the hour and scored 122. Goals were the smallest component error of the nine (+11 across the league); appearance points were the largest (+49). The model is good at what a footballer does and worse at whether he is on the pitch — which is a fixable problem and a different one.

Every player bucketed by the chance the model gave him of reaching the hour, against the share who actually did. A calibrated model tracks the diagonal. The bar is what happened; the tick is what was said.

Good in the middle, and it knows who is not playing — 185 players given under 10% and 10% of them featured for an hour. It is over-confident at the top: of the 95 given 90% or better, 82% made the hour, which is managers resting people and taking them off at 55. Expected 215 starts across the league, got 208.

03

The magazine

One story, told properly, with the rest as sidebars. GameweekCover.tsx already builds the week ahead as a front page — headline, captain story, a differential and a value pick — and nothing imports it. Pointed backwards it is a review: the gameweek had one story, and in GW1 it was that the most-owned player in the game blanked while the best eleven cost £63m and nobody owned it. For: it is the only direction with a voice, it is the most shareable, and the component is largely written. Against: a headline has to be chosen, and choosing it is a judgement the model cannot make — some weeks nothing happened and the page will strain to pretend otherwise.
Gameweek 1 · the story

Two thirds of the game captained Haaland. He scored two.

The best captain in gameweek 1 was a £4.5m Brighton left back nobody owned. That is not a recommendation — it is the size of the swing one disc carried: 34 points against 4. The eleven that actually returned cost £63.0m and averaged 8.1% ownership, and the model that ranks them all was out by 1.66 a player, almost entirely because it was wrong about who would be on the pitch rather than what they would do on it.

Your week first

The review nobody else can write is the one about you. The Team ID is already stored, the actuals are already joined on code, and My Team's board already knows your fifteen — so the page can open on your gameweek and put the league's behind it. Everything below the scoreboard is identical to direction 1. For: it is the only version anyone would open on a Monday night rather than read once, and it makes GW Review and My Team one product rather than two pages that both know your squad. Against: it is useless to a visitor who has not entered a Team ID, which on day one is everybody — so it needs a real answer for that state, not a form where the content should be.
