Mockups · GW Review, four visual arguments

Four ways the review could look, none of them a card grid

Same measured gameweek — 495 players projected, 310 who featured — drawn four different ways. The point of each is the form: what a chart, a page of type, a distribution or a printed slip each make obvious that a row of tiles cannot.

A

The scatter

One chart that contains the entire report card. Every player the model projected is a dot: what it said along the bottom, what he scored up the side. The gold diagonal is being exactly right. Everything above it was under-projected, everything below over-projected, and the shape of the cloud is the finding — a dense floor along the bottom because most footballers score one or two, and a thin column of hauls the model had no way to see coming. For: it is the most information per pixel the page could carry, it needs no words, and it is the same chart every week so the cloud tightening over a season is the model improving. Against: a scatter plot on a football site asks something of the reader, and the one thing it cannot show is who any of the dots are without labelling them by hand.
Goalkeepers Defenders Midfielders Forwards 495 players · gold line = perfect projection

The asymmetry is the whole story. Below the line the error is bounded: the most a projection can overshoot by is the number it named, and the highest number it named all week was 6.59. Above the line there is no ceiling, and gameweek one put six players more than ten points clear of what they were given. A projection names a median; the row of dots along the floor is where most footballers actually live, and the thin column above is what the tail costs. Three players finished below zero, which is why the axis starts at minus three.

B

The broadsheet

Set it like a newspaper, because that is what it is. A review is written, not dashboarded: a headline, a standfirst, three columns of type and a block of agate results at the foot — the classified scores a Saturday sports paper ran for a century because it is the densest legible way to print a lot of small numbers. For: it is the only direction with a voice, it reads as authored rather than generated, and it is unmistakably not another dark dashboard. Against: it breaks the site's own visual language on purpose, it needs real editing every week or it reads as filler, and justified type in three columns on a phone is one column of compromises.
The Gameweek
No. 1 · Season 2026/27 · Monday, late
The Review — Gameweek One

Two thirds of the game captained Haaland. He scored two.

The best eleven of the opening weekend cost sixty-three million pounds and almost nobody owned a single one of them.

The armband is the largest decision in fantasy football and gameweek one settled it against almost everybody. Erling Haaland, owned by 69.4 per cent of the game and captained by the great majority of them, played ninety minutes at home to Bournemouth and returned two points. Doubled, four.

The player who should have had it was a £4.5m left back at Brighton. Maxim De Cuyper scored, assisted, kept a clean sheet and took three bonus points for seventeen — thirty-four with the armband, and a thirty-point swing against the pick two thirds of the game made. Nobody was going to captain him. That is not the point. The point is the size of the number one disc was worth.

Six of the best eleven were owned by fewer than two managers in a hundred.

Behind him the pattern held. Hull City supplied three of the eleven — Tzolakis, Mendy and Ajayi, twelve and a half million between them and thirty-nine points — and Brentford another two. The whole side comes to sixty-three million against a hundred-million budget, and averages 8.1 per cent ownership.

This paper's own projections were out by 1.66 points a player across all 495 it named, and the direction of the error is worth publishing plainly: it was wrong about who would be on the pitch, not about what they would do once there. Sixteen players it rated three points or better did not appear at all. Forty-one it rated under one and a half played the hour and took a hundred and twenty-two points between them.

Goals were the smallest component error of nine. Appearance points were the largest. A model that knows what a footballer does and not whether he is playing has a fixable problem, and a different one from the model that cannot tell a good striker from a bad one.

C

The distribution

One number, one chart, one idea per screen. Not a summary of the gameweek — an argument about it. Every player who kicked a ball, sorted by what he scored, and the shape that comes out says more about fantasy football than any table: it is not a bell, it is a cliff with a tail. For: it is instantly legible on a phone, it is the most shareable thing on the list, and it gives the site a way to say something true and slightly counter-intuitive every single week. Against: one idea per screen means a lot of screens, and the idea has to actually be there — some gameweeks the distribution is the same distribution as last week and the page has nothing to say.

Fifty-five per cent of everyone who played managed two points or fewer. Nine per cent reached nine. Eight players in the entire league scored thirteen or more, and one reached seventeen. Every argument about captaincy, differentials and template is really an argument about that tail on the right — which is thin, which is the point, and which is why a projection that names a median will be wrong about it every week.

D

The receipt

Your gameweek, itemised, on a till slip. A review of your own team is a bill: this is what you ordered, this is what each item came to, this is what you paid, here is the total at the bottom. The form does the explaining — nobody needs to be told how to read a receipt, and the two lines reading 0.00 — did not play land harder printed on paper than they ever would in a table cell. For: it is the most shareable object the site could produce, it renders identically at any width, and it is a natural export — one image, no cropping. Against: it is a joke that has to stay funny for thirty-eight weeks, it holds about fifteen lines before it stops being a receipt, and there is nowhere to put a chart.
