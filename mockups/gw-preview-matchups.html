What each one costs the page

P1 adds about 260px between Captain and Every fixture on a desktop, and roughly 400 on a phone where the lanes stack. It is the only one that answers "who should I be looking at this week" without scanning ten fixtures, and it is nearly free to build — the Matchup Explorer already computes exactly this list, for one opponent at a time.

P2 fills the right-hand column of the expanded match-of-the-round, which is the one place on this page with room for it and an audience already reading detail. Below that card, though, the fixtures are half-width two-up and there is nowhere to put three rows — so P2 is really "match of the round only" unless every fixture card grows.

P3 adds one line, and only where the data has something to say. The Newcastle–Liverpool card above shows the case that matters: nothing added, no space taken. On a phone that silence is the whole argument for it.

Still P1 + P3. P1 is the section people would come back for; P3 makes the fixture list read like analysis rather than a table. P2 is the best-looking of the three and the one I would leave out — it only fits on one card in ten.

On moving P1 to the gap. The space is real — 427px, measured at 1440 and 1760 — and filling it costs the page no height at all, which the high placement does not. But the gap only exists on a desktop: a phone has one column, so there P1 lands about 5,000px down between two lists, and nobody scrolling for team news is looking for a shortlist. If it goes there, it wants a link from the top of the page — a line in The round at a glance saying "four players the fixture suits" that jumps to it — otherwise it is a good section almost nobody will meet.
