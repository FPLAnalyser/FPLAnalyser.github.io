Transfer planning, in plain English

You asked what I meant by “transfer planning is different”. Here it is with real numbers from your Plan A squad — no jargon.

The difference in one sentence

We compare destinations. We don’t plan routes.

Everything we have built compares finished squads — here are two teams, which one scores more. What it never does is work out the moves that get you there, and in which week to make them.

It’s the difference between a map and a satnav. The map shows you where places are. The satnav works out the route — and crucially, you only ever drive the next junction before it recalculates.

Why saving wins here, in one line: one transfer can only swap one player. Two transfers can do things one cannot — here it downgrades the goalkeeper to help pay for a much better midfielder. Saving a transfer isn’t doing nothing. It’s buying the option to do something bigger.

Nothing on the site answers this today. It is also the cheapest thing here to build, because it only has to look one week ahead.

The honest bit

+4.0 points sounds good until you see the scale

Your Compare tab already measured how much a six-week total bounces around. Plan A against Plan B came out to a mean gap of 3 points inside a range of −38 to +44. Here is where a +4.0-point plan sits on that same scale:

−38 · a bad run0 · no change+44 · a good run

The gold line is the plan’s entire six-week edge. Luck moves you ten times further than the plan does.

That does not make planning pointless — the edge is real and it is the part you control. It does mean a screen that prints “+4.0 points” in big letters is lying about how sure it is.

What I would build

The satnav, not the itinerary

BuildWhy
Use it or save itAnswers a question you have every week. Only looks one week ahead, so it is small and trustworthy.
This week’s move, in fullThe only thing you actually commit to. Search six weeks deep to choose it well — like a chess engine thinking ahead to pick one move.
Later weeks, greyed outShown so you can see where it is heading, drawn as provisional so nobody treats it as a promise.
“Beats holding …% of the time”A win rate rather than “+4.0 points” — the same paired simulation the Compare tab already runs. Not computed here: it needs the simulation, which is the build, not the mock-up.
What I would not build: a six-week plan presented as a plan, or a button that applies it for you. That version would look the most impressive and mislead the most people — and there is one more catch: this all assumes prices never change. In real life your budget drifts every week, so a plan that is £0.1m tight in GW5 may simply be impossible by the time you get there.
