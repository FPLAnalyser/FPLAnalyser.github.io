The Season Spine

Layout 1, running. The thing a still picture cannot show: the timeline and the plan grid are one object. The bars are the grid’s top row, so expanding does not swap views — it reveals the rows that were always underneath, on columns that never move.

  1. 1Tap a gameweek on the bars. The squad below changes to that week — fixtures, numbers and all.
  2. 2Tap Pull down for the whole plan. Fifteen rows slide in under the bars.
  3. 3With the plan open, flip Fixtures → xP to see every man’s projected points per week. The C on a cell is your armband; dim rows are that week’s bench. On the bars, a C means your squad holds the game’s best projected captain that week — no C, and the best pick is someone you don’t own.

Real numbers. The fifteen is a plan built in the live builder, the fixtures and difficulty ratings are this season’s, and the projections and hard-fixture counts are what the app produces for that squad. Only the two played weeks are invented, since the season has not started — they are there to show the past-and-future split the line is built on. One approximation: per-player xP in the xP view is derived here from rating and fixture — the weekly totals on the bars are the app’s real projections, and the live build would read both from the same model.

Why the table never grows. The rows are your fifteen squad slots, not players. A transfer is one row changing hands — the incoming name sits in the cell where he arrives, ringed green, and the lineage reads under the name on the left. A wildcard is the same thing eleven times in one column: a vertical seam, not eleven new rows. A free hit would be one column of temporary names that revert the following week. Fifteen rows in August, fifteen in May, however much you tinker.

What the expand would cost to build: nothing new. The bars are the gameweek strip that already exists. The rows are the Fixtures tab, already built, drawn on the strip’s columns instead of its own. The moves row is data the strip already computes. One CSS grid, one height animation.
