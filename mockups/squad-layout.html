Squad page — three layouts

The page has grown a toolbar, a gameweek grid, four stat tiles, a pitch, a bench and a player list, and reaching the bottom of your own team takes a scroll. Three ways to tighten it.

Idea A · smallest change

Controls onto the pitch

Rating / £ / xP is a view of the cards, so it belongs on the thing it changes. It moves to the pitch corner as three small buttons. Auto pick and Import are squad actions and stay in the toolbar, where a destructive-ish button should be.

The risk is discoverability — three 26px glyphs in a corner are easy to miss in a way a labelled pill is not, so they would want a one-time hint the first time the pitch renders. I would not move Auto pick there: it replaces fifteen players, and a button that big should never be a small icon floating over the thing it overwrites.

Idea B · my recommendation

Stats beside the pitch, not above it

The four tiles are a full row of vertical space spent on four numbers. Put them in the right-hand column beside the pitch, where the Squad Lab already lives, and the whole team fits above the fold.

This is the one I would build. It costs nothing conceptually — the numbers do not change, they move — and it buys back the row that currently pushes the forwards below the fold on a laptop. On a phone the column collapses underneath, which is the same as today, so nothing is lost where there was nothing to gain.

It also puts the numbers next to the thing they describe. A projection sitting above a gameweek strip reads as a header; sitting beside the pitch it reads as that team's score, which is what it is.

Idea C · biggest change

Pitch and list side by side

The real reason the page is long is that the player list lives underneath the pitch, so every pick is scroll down, choose, scroll up, check. Put them side by side and the loop closes.

Fixes the actual problem rather than the symptom: picking a fifteen is fifteen round trips, and this removes all of them. The cost is that it only works on a wide screen — below about 1100px there is no second column and the list has to go back underneath, so this is a desktop layout with the current one as the phone fallback. That is a real fork in the design, not a responsive tweak, and it is the reason I would do B first and hold this until B is in.

On forking a plan

Worth building, and cheap

Yes. “Fork from GW6” copies the plan, keeps every week up to GW6 and lets the copy diverge after it — which is exactly how you test “what if I wildcard here instead” without losing the plan you already trust.

Plan A 15/15 Duplicate Fork from GW6

Duplicate already exists and copies the whole plan; a fork is the same operation with a cut point, so the plumbing is done. The one design decision worth getting right is what the fork keeps: weeks up to and including the cut stay identical, everything after is cleared rather than copied. Copying the tail would produce two plans that look different and behave the same until you notice, which is the bug the duplicated-plans work already fixed once.

It also pairs with Compare: fork at the wall, play the wildcard in one and not the other, and the head-to-head is measuring exactly one decision.
