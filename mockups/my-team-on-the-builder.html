Mockups · My Team, built on the Squad Builder

My Team is the Squad Builder, with your fifteen already in it

Five ways to fit a real season into the board that already exists — the season spine, the gameweek stepper, the pitch and its fixture chips, the chip row, and the Players / Analysis / Fixtures / Risk column. Nothing below invents a component the Squad Builder does not already have. Every number is read off the running page with this fifteen loaded, or out of site_data/2026-27/. Only what is genuinely unknowable is stamped.

The idea the existing design hands you. The spine already draws one bar per gameweek, forwards. A real team has a past, so the same chart runs the other way too: bars behind now are what you scored, bars ahead are what you are projected, and the join is the gameweek in progress. Look back, live and plan forward stop being three tabs and become one chart you were already looking at. Four of the five directions below are arguments about how you move along it.
01

One spine, two halves

Nothing is added to the page at all. It is the Squad Builder, with your fifteen loaded from a Team ID instead of picked, and the spine extended backwards to GW1. Step the gameweek stepper into a week that has been played and every panel switches to its past tense on its own: the shirts carry points instead of xP, the tiles read scored instead of projected, and the right column's fifth tab is Review. For: one page, no new navigation, and the code is mostly wiring rather than building. Against: a page that silently changes meaning depending on the stepper is a page people can misread — and there is nowhere to stand that shows you the season rather than a week of it.

02

Track / Plan

One switch, and it says which way you are facing. Track is the season behind you and the one in front of you today — live points, rank, where the model missed. Plan is the Squad Builder exactly as it is now, on your real fifteen, with your real bank and free transfers. The spine is the same chart in both; only the half it emphasises changes. For: the two things genuinely have different moods, and one word tells you which you are in — no guessing what the stepper did. Against: a switch is a decision you have to make before the page will tell you anything, and the honest answer most of the time is "both".

03

Sub-tabs, the way the board already does it

The Squad Builder already has a tab row — Squad, Insights, Compare Plans — sitting above the season spine. My Team uses the same row, with the four faces you asked for: Live, Plan, Review, Season. The spine and the manager strip stay put above it, because they are true on every tab. For: it is the pattern the reader has already learned one page across, and each face gets room to be good rather than fighting for the same column. Against: four tabs is four places to be wrong about which one someone wants on a Saturday — and Season will be empty and faintly embarrassing until about November.

04

The spine is the navigation

No tabs and no stepper — the bars are the controls. The chart is already the only thing on the page that shows the whole season at once, so clicking a bar loads that week onto the board beneath it. Click GW1 and you get what happened; click GW2 and you get the deadline; click GW9 and you get a fixture read with nothing pretending to be a team sheet. The board changes what it offers depending on which bar you are standing on. For: it removes two navigation systems and replaces them with the one that carries the most information per pixel. Against: bars are small targets on a phone, and nothing about a bar chart says "I am clickable" until you try.

05

The grid is the front door

The Squad Builder's best screen is hidden behind "pull down for the whole plan". Every man, every week, one cell each — and for a real team that grid has a past: the cells behind now hold what each player actually scored, the cells ahead hold the fixture. One screen, fifteen players, thirteen weeks, no navigation and no scrolling to compare. For: it is the densest true thing the site can draw, it makes a bad run visible without reading a word, and the component exists. Against: it is close to unusable on a phone, and opening on a wall of numbers is a hard first impression for someone who has just typed in a Team ID.

What is real. The manager strip's rank, total and free transfers come from the FPL entry endpoint via your Team ID and are stamped here — nothing in the repo fetches them yet, and that is the one piece of plumbing all five of these need. Everything else is measured: the spine's GW2–13 numbers are the ones the running Squad Builder draws for this exact fifteen; the ratings, prices, fixture chips and risk rows are read off the same page; GW1's points are the real ones from actuals/gw1.json, provisional while bonus settles.
