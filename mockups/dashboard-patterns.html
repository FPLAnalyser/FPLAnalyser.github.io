That dashboard, translated

Four patterns worth stealing from the security console, each rebuilt around a question an FPL manager actually has. Every figure is computed from today's site_data using the fifteen from Plan A.

The thing that console does well is not the dark theme — it is that every panel is about triage. It never shows you a number without telling you whether to act on it. That is the transferable idea; the visual language is just how it gets there.

Idea 1 · from the tactics heatmap

Your fifteen against the next eight

Their grid is tactic × day. Ours is player × gameweek, coloured by fixture difficulty — so a rough patch is a shape you see rather than a number you compute. The right-hand column counts hard games in the next six.

This is the panel the site is most obviously missing. Fixtures already exist per club and Squad Lab already counts hard weeks for the squad as a whole — but nobody plans transfers at squad level, they plan them per player. Reading down a column tells you which week hurts; reading across a row tells you who to sell, and those are different questions that currently need two screens.

The segmented bar is worth stealing on its own — twelve blocks read as a quantity at a glance where a percentage reads as a fact to be checked, and it degrades gracefully at phone width.

The composite here is deliberately simple and stated rather than hidden: the chance he fails to reach 60 minutes, plus hard fixtures in the next six, plus a penalty if he is trending down in price. I would not ship it as an opaque "risk score". The reason their version works is that "Privilege · 92%" has one meaning; a made-up FPL risk number has as many meanings as there are people reading it, so the Driver column has to name which signal is doing the work.

Idea 3 · from the alert center

Since you last looked

Severity chip, one line, a timestamp, and a way to see everything. The site currently makes you go and find news; this brings it to you, scoped to the players you actually own.

The honest constraint: "since you last looked" needs to know when you last looked, and the site has no accounts. localStorage can hold a timestamp and that is enough for one browser — but it means the feed is per-device, and a reader on a new phone sees everything as new. Worth building, worth saying so on the panel rather than pretending it is a synced inbox.

Where your points actually come from

This one surprised me, which is usually the sign a chart is worth having. Appearance points are 39% of the projected GW1 total — the largest single block, bigger than goals. Not a quirk of this squad: eleven players turning up is 22 points before anyone does anything, and it reframes what a "safe" pick is. It also explains why the head-to-head sim lands so close to 50/50 so often, since that floor is identical in every plan and only the thin layers on top are actually being chosen.

What I would not take. The KPI strip, the date-range picker and the export button belong to a console someone watches all day; FPL has one deadline a week, and a dashboard that implies constant monitoring would be inventing urgency to fill a layout. The same goes for the severity language — "Critical" on a fantasy football fixture is the tone that makes a site feel like it is selling you something. Ideas 1 and 2 are the ones I would build first: they answer questions the site currently cannot, and neither needs a single new number in the pipeline.
