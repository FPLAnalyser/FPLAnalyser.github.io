FPL Analyser — layout study

Make the squad the subject

Three directions for the same problem: people are building draft squads and want them judged. Measured today — the Squad Builder page is 9,417px on a phone, 11.2 screens, the full rating breakdown is behind a modal, and 424 lines of finished analysis (SquadInsights, TeamPlanning) are mounted on no page at all.

Direction A — your idea 1

Home becomes the dashboard

The squad lives on the left and never leaves; every tile on the right is an analysis of it rather than a section of a website. Strongest personalisation. The cost is the empty state: every first visit, and every visit from search, opens on a page asking for something the visitor hasn't got.

Direction C — the recommendation

Home knows whether you have a squad; the builder holds the depth

Not a third design — B, plus a home page with two states. No squad and it is today's front door with one strong invitation. A squad and the top of home becomes the verdict and the three things to fix, with the tiles below reframed as analyses of your team. Personalisation without an empty dashboard, and without two places showing your XI.

Cost, honestly

What each one takes, 19 days out

B — do first
Mostly re-arrangement. SquadRatingSheet comes out of its modal, SquadInsights and TeamPlanning get mounted at last, the lab's contents become the tiles. Little new logic.
C — then this
One new component and a branch on "do we have fifteen". Reuses B's verdict card wholesale.
A — not before launch
Rebuilds the front door and needs an empty state designed for six locked tiles. High risk for the one page every visitor sees.

Everything above draws on analysis that already exists and mostly is not on screen. The percentile — "better than 78% of squads built here" — is the one genuinely new number, and it needs a distribution to compare against: run the rating over a few thousand generated legal squads once, ship the percentile curve as a static file.
