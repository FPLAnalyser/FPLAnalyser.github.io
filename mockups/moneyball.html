Moneyball for FPL — the whole layer

Four ideas, all on this repo's own data: what is skill and what is luck, what a player is worth above free, where the market misprices that, and what a transfer has to earn. Reliability comes from 11,491 Understat and 15,183 Premier League per-match rows for 2025/26; value comes from the ratings pipeline and the replacement pool.

1 · skill or luck — when does a metric start predicting itself
The heart of sabermetrics was not "use statistics", it was working out which numbers repeat. Each line splits a player's season into odd and even matches and correlates the two halves, so both cover the same stretch of season. Where a line crosses r = 0.5 is roughly where the metric starts describing the player rather than the sample. Big chances created needs about 15 matches. Touches in the opposition box needs 3. That is the whole lesson in one chart: the eye-catching stat is the noisy one, and the boring volume stat is the signal. Rate players on what crosses early; treat what crosses late as something to regress, not to chase.

2 · points above replacement

Sabermetrics never asked "how good is he", it asked "how much better than what I could have for nothing". The floor here is the best genuine starter at each position's minimum price — you can always field these, so nothing below them is worth paying for.

PAR = points per game above that position's replacement. Per £m divides it by spend above the floor price, which is the number a budget actually trades in. The GKP and FWD floors rest on 2 and 1 qualifying candidates, so those two baselines are thin.

3 · where the market misprices it

The same players as geometry. Across is price, up is points per game, dashed lines are replacement level for each position. Distance above your own dashed line is what you are actually buying; distance left is what you are paying. Faded dots return less than 0.3 points per £m above the floor — most of the league, which is the point. A defender clearing the green line by two points at £6m is a different purchase from a forward barely clearing red at £9m.

4 · what a hit has to earn back

A transfer is only free once a week; beyond that it costs 4. So the question is never "is he better", it is how many gameweeks until better pays back four points — and whether you will still own him by then. Green bars pay for themselves inside four gameweeks.

Uses points-above-replacement per game as the edge, which assumes the man you are dropping is replacement level. Swapping between two good players is a smaller edge and a longer payback.
