Scale changes what the view has to do. Six weeks can be a table. Thirty-eight cannot — three plans is 114 gameweeks of detail, and nobody reads that. The strip is deliberately almost content-free: it shows when you act, not what you did, because at season scale the rhythm is the plan. Everything else is a drill-down.

This is the number the category refuses to print. Every season planner on the market is built to make you feel that the difference between plans is the difference between seasons. On these projections it is 5.7 points in 2147 — a fifth of a single good captaincy week, spread over nine months. A comparison feature that cannot say that is marketing.
Divergence

How far apart the plans ever get

Running difference from the Anchor plan. The grey cone is the spread two identical plans would show anyway, from luck alone, widening with the square root of gameweeks played.

Early pivot vs AnchorChurn vs Anchorrange a coin-flip season would produce
The lines never leave the cone. Every plan spends the season inside the range that pure variance would produce between two copies of the same plan — so at no point in 38 weeks is the difference between them distinguishable from noise. Drawing the cone is what turns a comparison chart from a scoreboard into an honest instrument, and it is one line of maths nobody else puts on the screen.

Every one of the top four is a chip week. GW33 and GW15 are the two plans' Bench Boosts, GW12 and GW30 their Triple Captains — worth 15.4 and 13.4 points against a whole season of transfer decisions worth 5.7. That is the actionable conclusion of the entire exercise: at season scale the only decisions with real leverage are the chips, so a season comparison should lead with chip placement and treat transfer policy as texture. Every planner I have seen does the opposite.
Convergence

Plans agree more than they look like they do

Players shared between Anchor and Early pivot, every second gameweek. Fifteen of fifteen at the start.

GW2GW38
They never really part. Two plans built on different wildcard timing still hold 12 of 15 players by the end of the season, because both are chasing the same projections and converge on the same answers. Worth drawing precisely because it is counter-intuitive: the feeling of a plan is that it is yours, and the measurement is that it is mostly everyone's.
Honesty

What a 38-week projection is and is not

Priced by the market: 1 week

Bookmakers have GW1. Everything from GW2 is our own team strength, which is why the accumulating odds fit matters more than any planning feature.

Blanks and doubles: unknown

All 38 gameweeks currently have exactly 10 fixtures. The cup rounds that create the blanks and doubles a chip plan is built around have not been drawn. Any chip placed beyond about GW20 today is a placeholder.

Squad turnover: ignored

The planner assumes today's prices and no injuries. Neither survives contact with a season, and both matter more than the transfer policy being compared.

So what is a season plan for? Not prediction. It is for finding the weeks that will need a decision, and for placing chips where the fixture structure — not the projection — says they belong. The comparison is worth building because it makes that distinction visible: the strip shows where you act, the cone shows that most of it does not matter, and the decision points show the four weeks that do. That is a genuinely different product from a solver that hands you an optimum and lets you believe it.
