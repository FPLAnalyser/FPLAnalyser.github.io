1 · the captain board
Expected points is the wrong sort key on its own. Captaining the player 42% of the field captains is the neutral move — you gain nothing and lose nothing. The column that matters is edge: your captain's return minus what the field's captain is expected to return (10.40). Ceiling and floor sit beside the mean because doubling a score doubles its variance, and the right captain when you are chasing is not the right one when you are protecting a lead.

2 · risk and reward

The same ten as a shape. Across is expected captain points, up is the chance of a haul, bubble size is effective ownership, and the gold line is the field. Anything left of the line needs a reason. Bubbles that are small and high are the differentials — low effective ownership with real ceiling, which is exactly the trade you want when you are behind and exactly the one to avoid when you are ahead.

When to spend the chip, and when to hold it.

3 · Triple Captain as an option, not a treat

A chip is a one-time option, and the mistake is spending it below its value because it is burning a hole. Triple Captain adds one further multiple of the captain, so its worth in any week is simply the best captain expectation going. Gold bars are weeks that beat every week left after them — the red dashed line is the best remaining opportunity, so playing where the bar clears the line is the stopping rule. On this half of the season that is GW1, GW17 and GW19; everything between is a week to hold.

Bench Boost and Free Hit price the same way — against the bench's expected return and the gap between your XI and the best available XI — but both need the blank and double calendar, which is not published this far out.
