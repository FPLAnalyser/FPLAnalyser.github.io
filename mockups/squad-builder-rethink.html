Squad Builder — if we started again

Everything below is built and working. That is the problem: it was added one good idea at a time, and nobody has ever laid the whole thing out on a table and asked what shape it wants to be. This does that — the inventory first, then four ways to arrange it, then the one I would build.

First, honestly

Read that list as a stranger would. It is not one page — it is a picker, a planner and an analysis suite sharing a URL, and the reason it feels crowded is that all three are always on, whichever one you came for. Nothing below deletes anything. Every idea is about when each of the three is on.

Idea A · smallest change

Insights and Compare stop being tabs

They are already separate jobs; they are just wearing the builder's clothes. Give them their own routes and the builder page has one job again. The tab row becomes a link, and that is the whole change.

Idea B · my recommendation

One board, three modes

The page has three moods and pretends it has one. Pick is what you do once — an empty pitch and a list. Plan is the week-by-week work. Read is what you do when you have stopped changing things. Each mode shows only its own furniture, and the board is the one thing all three keep.

Read is the only mode where the board is not the point, so it stops being the biggest thing on screen. This is also where Insights belongs — Ladder, Shape, Outcomes and Decisions are readings of a finished squad, and a separate tab for them was only ever because there was nowhere else to put them.

Why this one. It is the only idea here that reduces what is on screen rather than moving it: nine modules above the board becomes three in Pick and four in Plan. It needs no new components — every panel already exists — and the mode is inferable, so it can default correctly: no squad → Pick, squad but nothing planned → Plan, and you only ever click Read on purpose.

Idea C · most ambitious

A workspace that does not scroll

Fix the board on the left, dock everything else in a scrolling rail on the right, and let the page be exactly one screen tall — the way a desktop tool behaves rather than a document.

The appeal: the team is always in front of you, and every number you change updates in view — right now you can be scrolled to the market with the board off screen, changing a projection you cannot see. The risk: a fixed viewport is brutal on a 13-inch laptop, and everything currently below the fold has to justify a tab. It also fights the phone, where a fixed board and a dock is two thirds of a screen of chrome.

Idea D

The board answers, and the panels come to it

No tabs at all. Every panel is summoned by the thing it is about: tap a player for his card, tap an empty shirt for the market filtered to that position, tap a gameweek in the strip for that week's fixtures, tap the rating for the breakdown. The right column disappears entirely and the board goes full width.

This is the phone layout, promoted. It is the cleanest thing on this page and the worst on a large screen: a 1440 monitor becomes a 680px column with 760px of black either side, and browsing a market list in a sheet over the board is worse than a list beside it. Worth stealing the idea, not the layout — the taps should all work in whichever arrangement wins.

What I'd build

A, then B

Ship A first — Insights and Compare become their own routes this afternoon, and the page immediately stops carrying two things it is not for. It is reversible and it costs nothing.

Then B, one mode at a time, starting with Pick, because that is the mode with the worst ratio today: eleven modules on screen to help you do a thing none of them can comment on until you have finished it. Plan is nearly right already. Read absorbs Insights, which is where the second half of idea A lands.

I would take one thing from C — the board should not scroll out of view while you are changing it — and solve it with a sticky board rather than a fixed shell, which gets the benefit without the 13-inch problem. And I would take the taps from D regardless of which layout wins.
