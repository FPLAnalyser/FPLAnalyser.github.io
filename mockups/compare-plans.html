Compare plans — a rethink

The page compares two fifteens at gameweek one. But a plan is not a fifteen, it is a route: transfers, captains and chips across a run of weeks. Two plans can hold the same fifteen in GW1 and be completely different bets by GW6 — and the current page shows that difference as a chart underneath two team sheets, which is the wrong way round. Below: the frame, four concepts that would replace the body of the page, and three that are further out. Numbers are illustrative but internally consistent, so the concepts can be read against each other.

The decision this page exists to serve. Not "which fifteen is better" — "which route should I take, and when do I find out I was wrong". That reframes everything: the unit is the gameweek, not the squad; the interesting weeks are the ones where the plans disagree; and the answer changes with how far ahead you look, which is why the horizon has to be a control rather than a fixed six.

The hit is priced in. The horizon is the argument.
Why this is not a filter. A six-week window is a house assumption and it decides the answer. Showing the same two plans at 4, 6, 8 and 10 turns "Plan 2 is better" into "Plan 2 is better if you hold it past GW4", which is the actual claim and the one you can act on. A plan that only wins at ten weeks is a plan you must not wildcard away at six.

Replace the body

2 · The crossover — one line, not two core

Cumulative difference, week by week. Above the line, Plan 2 leads. The moment the lead changes hands is the whole story.

Plan 2 pays four points in GW2 and is 3.4 behind. It has that back within a week and is clear from GW4. If your wildcard is GW5, this is not a decision you get to keep — the payoff lands after you would have torn it up.
Why one line beats two. Two cumulative lines both climb from 0 to ~330 and sit on top of each other; a 5-point gap on a 330-point axis is invisible. Plotting the difference spends the whole vertical axis on the only quantity anyone is choosing between, and the zero crossing becomes a real event on the page instead of something you work out by subtracting two labels.

Is it duplication? No — the axis is different. The Squad Builder's spine is one plan × fifteen players; this is N plans × weeks. The player rows are what you cannot show here without drowning: two fifteens over ten weeks is 300 cells and nobody reads it. What survives the compression is the week's total, the armband and the moves — which is exactly the plan, and exactly what the current page throws away. A row per plan also scales to three and four plans, which two pitches never will.

Further out

6 · The bet, in one sentence out there

Reduce the comparison to the marginal decision and state its break-even.

You are paying 4 points now to hold Gyökeres two weeks earlier than Plan 1 does.
He has to out-score Watkins by 4.0 across GW2–GW3 to break even.
The model has him beating Watkins by 6.8 over those two weeks.
Break-even needs him to hit 59% of his projection. He has cleared that in 4 of his last 6.
Why this might be the whole page. Every comparison of two plans that differ by one move reduces to a break-even, and a break-even is a sentence, not a chart. It also fails loudly and usefully: when two plans differ by six players there is no single sentence, and the page should say so and fall back to the log.

7 · What has to happen for you to be wrong out there

Invert the projection. Instead of a point estimate, the conditions under which the other plan wins.

Plan 1 wins the six weeks if any one of these happens:
Gyökeres blanks in both of GW2 and GW331% likely
Watkins returns in 3 of 6 (he is projected 2)28% likely
Arsenal's GW4 fixture moves to a blanklow, but it is a cup week
Taken together: Plan 1 wins about a third of the time. A 5.4-point projected gap is a lean, not a certainty — but you are choosing the lean.

9 · Same route, different order out there

A question the current page cannot ask: what if the two plans make the same moves in a different order?

Both plans sign Gyökeres and Semenyo and sell Watkins and Gordon. Plan 2 does it in GW2 and GW5; Plan 1 in GW4 and GW3. The squads are identical by GW6 and the fifteens are indistinguishable at the end of the window.
So the page should say "this is the same plan, ordered differently" and compare only the ordering — two hits versus one, and which fixtures each man is owned for. Detecting that case is cheap (compare the end-state fifteens) and it changes the entire framing of the answer.

What I would build

1. Horizon control + the headline gap, with the flip called out — the frame (#1)
2. The crossover chart — where the lead changes hands (#2)
3. The divergence log — what actually differs, week by week (#3)
4. The two timelines — the plans as routes, scaling to 3 and 4 plans (#4)
5. The squad difference, with both fifteens behind a click — (#5)
Then #6 (the bet) as the headline sentence whenever the plans differ by a single move, which is most of the time, and #9 as a guard that catches "same plan, different order" before the page draws anything. #7 and #8 are worth a try but neither is load-bearing.
