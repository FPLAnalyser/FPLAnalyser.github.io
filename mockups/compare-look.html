The thing the reference site is doing is not mainly the green. Its ground is a cool blue-grey, not near-black, and every panel sits a step lighter than the page. That is why it reads as bright and modern — and it is why Pitch above looks like a different product while Verdant, which changes only the accent, still looks like ours in a different colour. Changing the accent is one line. Changing the ground is a proper piece of work: nine surface tokens, both light and dark, and every share image re-checked because the export canvas paints its own background.
Against changing it at all: the gold is what the wordmark, every share card and the domain identity are built on, and a site that looks unlike its own screenshots is a worse trade than a slightly warmer background. For it: nothing about the accent is load bearing on a dark ground, Frost and Verdant are already token-complete, and offering the picker costs one array. My recommendation is to turn the picker back on first — that is free and you can live with each for a day — and treat the Pitch surface as a separate decision after.

What I would take from their version, and what I would not. Take: the two named slots with a swap, the horizon control, and above all the strip of tiles that each name a winner — that is the same instinct as our head-to-head panel and it belongs at the top rather than four scrolls down. Leave: their tiles are all point estimates, so five of them agree with each other and none says how sure it is. Ours has the one number theirs cannot print — 63% of paired runs — and that is the tile the whole screen should be built around.
Compare · three

The timeline, as a line

What you asked for. Projected points per week for each plan, and underneath, the running gap — which is the line that actually answers "when does this decision pay off".

Balanced Haaland build projected points per gameweek

Running gap — Balanced minus Haaland build

The second chart is the one worth having. Six weeks of two wobbling lines is a picture of variance; the running gap is a picture of the decision. It says the difference is made almost entirely in GW2 and GW4 — Balanced is ahead by 1.6 after one week and by 7.9 after two — and that the Haaland build wins GW3 and GW5 outright, which is the whole argument for holding him through a bad opening. A total of "+11.7" says none of that.
One honest limit to keep on the chart: this compares two fixed fifteens with a best legal eleven each week. It does not know about the transfers you will make in week three. Over six weeks that is a reasonable read; over a season it is not, which is why the season version of this belongs with the 38-week planner rather than here.

Formations differ, so the pitches are not row-aligned — Balanced fields 3-4-3 in GW1 and the Haaland build 4-4-2, because £15.5m in one striker has to come out of the back line. Two lists could never show you that; two pitches show it before you read a number.
Order I would build these in: the A/B pickers and the decision strip first — they change the whole screen for about a day's work and reuse numbers we already compute. The two charts next, because you asked for them and the running gap is the best thing on this page. The two pitches last: they need the per-week lineups threaded into the comparison, which is real plumbing rather than layout.
